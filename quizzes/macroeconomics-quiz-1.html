Name ________________________________________________

1) Macroeconomics primarily involves the study of:

a) individual firms

b) the national and global economy

c) very large products, such as giant boulders

d) choices individuals make about savings

2) In economics, inflation generally means:

a) an increase in unemployment

b) a decline in many or most prices

c) the process by which balloons and whatnot are inflated

d) a rise in the price of many or most goods

3) In economics, a positive conclusion is one:

a) that does not involve the value judgments of the person reaching the conclusion

b) that is optimistic about the future of the economy

c) that is almost never called for

d) that declares unemployment will drop

4) In making economic decisions, we may have to trade off rising living standards against:

a) unemployment

b) inflation

c) productivity

d) the desire for stability

5) Government stimulus spending during a recession would be recommended by:

a) Say's Law

b) classical economics

c) Keynesian economics

d) Adam Smith

6) Say's Law could be summarized as:

a) "Say what?!"

b) "Supply is demand."

c) "Government spending is key!"

d) "Whip inflation now."

7) "Economic development" refers to:

a) the creation of better economic theories

b) a nation moving from poverty to greater wealth

c) hitting the lotto and getting rich

d) classical economics

8) "Laissez faire" refers to:

a) an economic policy that leaves the market alone

b) the great French economist who formulated Say's Law

c) a ménage à trois

d) full government control of the means of production

9) The single event that most caused people to question classical economics was:

a) the Dreyfus Affair

b) World War I

c) the 1927 World Series

d) the Great Depression

10) A "school of thought" refers to:

a) a school where philosophy and mathematics are studied, as opposed to schools of body, like medical school

b) a school that makes you think twice about going there

c) a number of thinkers who adopt a similar approach to a subject

d) don't choose 'd' for this question -- it's not even an answer!
