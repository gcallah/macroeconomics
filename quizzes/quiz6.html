Macroeconomics: Quiz 6

Name: _________________________________________

Write your answers ON THE QUIZ ITSELF, except for any graphs and tables. Show your work on the back of the quiz or on graph paper: if you are on target except for a math error, you will get some credit.

1) Carefully draw and label a supply-and-demand diagram for the classical loanable funds market. Assuming that the market starts and ends in equilibrium, indicate what happens if there is a sudden INCREASE in businesses’ need for loans.

a) Which curve shifts and in which direction?

b) What happens to the equilibrium interest rate?

c) What happens to the equilibrium amount of investment?

2) Which of the following is most likely to represent a case of excessive inventory accumulation:

a) A sporting goods store has a buildup of swimming suits just before the start of the summer.

b) A manufacturer of backhoes sees backhoes piling up at the factory.

3) Suppose the relationship between consumption and income is C = 20 + .80Y.

a) For each additional dollar households receive, how much do they save?

b) Make a table like 9.2 in the text, and fill in all cells for Y = 0, 100, 200, and 300.

c) Graph this consumption function, along with a 45 degree “consumption=income” line. Show how visually we can see the level of savings when income equals 300.

4) What happens in a Keynesian model if households decide to CONSUME MORE?

a) Suppose the economy initially is in the situation C = 5 + .75Y and II = 70. Graph the resulting AD curve. Label the curve AD0, add the 45-degree line, and label the equilibrium point E0. (You might set up a table like 9.3 to help with this. Fill out rows for Y = 0, 100, 200, 300, and 400.)

b) What is the equilibrium level of income in this initial case?

c) Now suppose people decide they want to spend more and save less, so that the new consumption function is C = 30 + .75Y. Graph this function and label it AD1. (Make a new table like 9.3 to help you do this.)

d) What is the new equilibrium point? Label it E1.
